Thanks to my ever hardworking teammates, Ilef and Farhan, we were able to come up with a vision for something that affects us all but almost nobody stopped to pay attention.

"In this hackathon, most teams will naturally go for education apps or health apps. Those are safe topics, but they’re also very crowded. Judges will see ten chatbots for learning, ten mental health assistants, ten medical dashboards. Even good ideas risk getting lost because they all solve problems people already expect.

Our idea is different: green digital. Almost no one thinks about the environmental impact of digital behavior, even though we all live online every day. That alone gives us a strong strategic advantage we’re not competing in a saturated space.

What makes this project really strong is that it solves a real but invisible problem. People care about the environment, but they only think about plastic, transport or electricity at home. Nobody thinks about emails, cloud storage, streaming, or screen time as pollution yet data centers consume massive energy and water.

Our app makes this invisible impact visible, understandable, and actionable. That’s real added value, not just awareness.

The app itself is designed to be simple on the surface, but powerful in impact. When a user opens it for the first time, we avoid the usual friction of asking for permissions, account connections, or tracking data, which people often refuse.
Instead, everything starts with an AI chatbot conversation. The chatbot acts like a guide, asking clear and concrete questions about the user’s daily digital habits things they can easily verify on their phone settings without any technical knowledge.

For example, it asks about average screen time per day, how many emails they usually receive or send, how much cloud storage they use for photos and videos, how often they stream content and at what quality, and how many devices they use daily.

This approach is important because it keeps privacy intact while still collecting enough meaningful data to work with.

Once the user submits their answers, the AI processes this information using environmental estimation models that translate digital activity into energy consumption, carbon emissions, and water usage.

What’s important here is that we’re not just giving raw numbers , the AI contextualizes them. Instead of saying “you used X kWh,” it explains what that actually means in real life.

And this is where the experience becomes truly impactful. Rather than using classic charts or graphs, which most people quickly ignore, the app transforms these values into human-scale, real-world equivalents.

The user sees things like how many homes their digital activity could power for a certain number of days, how much water is required to cool the data centers supporting their usage, or symbolic comparisons such as how long EIFFEL TOWER could stay lit using that same energy.

These comparisons make the impact tangible and emotionally understandable, not abstract. It educate them in a way that stays in their mind.
Then comes the most important part: action. The AI generates a personalized to-do list with simple digital actions the user can take like deleting duplicate files, reducing video quality, cleaning email subscriptions, limiting background apps and it is all personalized based on what they answered in the beginning.

When the user marks an action as done, the system instantly recalculates the impact and shows what they saved in the graphs. This creates engagement and motivation, not guilt. The user learns, acts, and sees results immediately.

What I really like about this project is that it fits Track 1 perfectly without being obvious. It touches environment, education, health, and community behavior at the same time."-Ilef
